Hooking Claude up to Mailchimp

A connector is just a link between two accounts. Set it up once, and Claude can build the email inside Mailchimp for you instead of you copying and pasting.

  • 1In Claude, open Settings, then Connectors

    Look through the list for Mailchimp and turn it on.

  • 2Sign in to Mailchimp when it asks, and say yes

    That’s the whole setup. You only do it once.

  • 3Ask Claude what lists you have

    Type: “What audiences and groups are in my Mailchimp?” Now you know the exact names to use.

  • 4Ask for a draft — never a send

    Claude builds it and stops. You open Mailchimp, look it over yourself, and press send.

  • 5Can’t find Mailchimp in the connector list?

    No problem at all. Copy the email out of the chat and paste it into a normal Mailchimp campaign. Same result, one extra step.

What to type once it’s connected

Using my Mailchimp account, please create a DRAFT campaign for me.

Audience: [Past clients]
Group or segment: [Building managers]
Subject line: use option 2 above
Body: use the HTML version above
From name: Chris Baker
Reply-to: [chris@bakersfloorandsurface.com]

Save it as a draft. Do not send it. Then tell me what you created and how
many people are in that group.

Before you hit send

  • 01Make sure it comes from “Chris Baker”

    Not “Baker’s Floor & Surface.” People open notes from people they’ve met.

  • 02Point replies at an inbox you check

    Replies are the entire point of this. Don’t let them land somewhere nobody looks.

  • 03Send yourself a test and read it on your phone

    Mailchimp has a “send a test email” button. That’s where your clients will read it, standing in a hallway.

  • 04Look for blanks

    Use Mailchimp’s preview with a real contact. If it opens “Hi ,” with no name, something didn’t fill in.

  • 05Leave the unsubscribe link in

    It has to be there by law, and it protects the email address you send estimates from.

  • 06Only send to people who know you

    Past clients only. Never a bought list.

Then set the rhythmFour times a year, each with a real reason: February before the rain, May to clean up after it, August ahead of the holiday crowds, and October when they’re setting next year’s budget.
