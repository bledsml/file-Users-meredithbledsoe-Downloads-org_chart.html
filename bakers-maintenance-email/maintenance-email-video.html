STEP 1

The prompt to paste into Claude

Change anything in [brackets]. Everything else is already true about Baker’s, so Claude doesn’t have to guess and won’t invent claims you can’t back up.

Draft the email

Help me write one short email to past clients of my company.

ABOUT US
Baker's Floor & Surface, San Mateo, California. Family-run, founded 1989.
Commercial surface contractor: concrete grinding, polishing, sealing and
staining; decorative concrete and micro-toppings; stone restoration,
maintenance, facade and veneer work, historic stone preservation; metal and
wood refinishing; graffiti and vandalism removal. Union-trained technicians.
We work with general contractors, building owners and facility managers in
San Francisco, Oakland, San Jose and the rest of the Bay Area.
Shop phone: 650-652-9440.

WHAT I'M OFFERING
Our Preventative Maintenance Program - a scheduled maintenance cadence for
surfaces we've already installed or restored, so they hold their finish
instead of needing a full restoration in a few years.

WHO IT'S GOING TO
[general contractors we've worked with in the last three years]

WRITE ME
1. Three subject lines, under 45 characters. No exclamation marks, no
   "don't miss out", no all-caps.
2. One email under 180 words that sounds like I typed it myself between
   jobs - plain, direct, no marketing voice. It should:
   - open by naming the work we did for them, using merge tags
   - say concretely what goes wrong when a surface isn't maintained
     (grit tracked in at entries, wrong-pH cleaners, over-aggressive pads)
   - explain the Preventative Maintenance Program in one sentence
   - end with ONE ask: I'll review what we installed for them and send
     back a suggested cadence and a number, free, no obligation
3. A one-line P.S. with our phone number.

Use these merge tags: *|FNAME|*, *|COMPANY|*, *|PROPERTY|*, *|SERVICE|*,
*|MONTH_YEAR|*.
Give me both a plain-text version and a simple HTML version.

Then show me the same email rewritten for owners and facility managers,
who care about budget and downtime rather than the schedule.

Then, once Mailchimp is connected

Using my Mailchimp account: create a DRAFT campaign in the [Past Clients]
audience, segment [Facility managers], with subject line 2 and the HTML
version above. From name: Chris Baker. Reply-to: [chris@bakersfloorandsurface.com].
Save it as a draft - do not send it. Then tell me exactly what you created
and how many contacts are in that segment.
STEP 2

The email, ready to edit

If Claude hands you something that sounds like a brochure, tell it: “cut the marketing voice, make it sound like a contractor wrote it in five minutes.” Here’s the version to aim for.

  • SUBJ 1Before your floors start showing it
  • SUBJ 2Quick question about *|PROPERTY|*
  • SUBJ 3Your *|SERVICE|* is due for a look

Body copy

Hi *|FNAME|*,

We finished the *|SERVICE|* at *|PROPERTY|* back in *|MONTH_YEAR|*. Hope it's
still doing its job.

Most of the surfaces we install don't fail - they get quietly ground down.
Grit tracked in at the entries. The wrong cleaner on stone. A scrubber pad
one grade too aggressive. Two or three years of that and a floor that could
have been maintained needs to be restored instead, which costs a lot more
and puts the space out of service while we do it.

That's why we run Preventative Maintenance Programs: a scheduled visit on a
cadence that matches your traffic, so the surface keeps its finish instead
of getting rescued.

If it's useful, I'll look at what we installed for *|COMPANY|* and send back
a suggested cadence and a number. No charge, no obligation - just reply and
tell me which building.

Chris Baker
President, Baker's Floor & Surface
Serving San Francisco, Oakland, San Jose and the Bay Area since 1989

P.S. Easier to talk it through? The shop is 650-652-9440.
STEP 3

Connecting Mailchimp to Claude

This is the part that turns a draft in a chat window into a campaign in your account. Do it once; it stays connected.

  • 1Open Settings › Connectors in Claude

    Browse the available connectors and add Mailchimp. You’ll sign in to Mailchimp once and approve the access.

  • 2Point it at the right audience

    Ask Claude to list your audiences and segments first, so you know the exact names to use in the next prompt.

  • 3Ask for a draft, never a send

    Claude builds the campaign and stops. You open Mailchimp, look at it with your own eyes, and press send yourself.

  • 4No connector on your plan? Nothing is lost

    Copy the HTML version out of the chat, paste it into a normal Mailchimp campaign. Same ten minutes, one extra paste.

STEP 4

Pre-flight, every time

  • 01From name: Chris Baker

    Not the company. It’s a note from a person they’ve met on a jobsite.

  • 02Reply-to is an inbox someone reads today

    Replies are the entire return on this email. Don’t send them to a black hole.

  • 03Test send, opened on your phone

    That’s where a facility manager will read it, standing in a mechanical room.

  • 04Every merge tag resolves

    Mailchimp’s preview mode with a real contact will show you any blanks. Set default values for the ones you can’t fill.

  • 05Unsubscribe link intact, list is people who know you

    Past clients only. Never a purchased list — it burns the domain you send estimates from.

  • 06Send Tuesday around 7:45 AM, then repeat quarterly

    February, May, August, October — each one tied to a real reason, not just “checking in.”
