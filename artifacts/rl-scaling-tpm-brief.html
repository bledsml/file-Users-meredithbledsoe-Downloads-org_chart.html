First 90 days

§2 · Ramp

Built on one assumption: a TPM who installs process before understanding the research loses the room permanently. So the first month adds nothing.

30Learn the loop
  • Shadow one scaling run end to end — SFT init, data workflow, training, eval, decision.
  • Map the RL workstream graph and where it touches data pipelines, evals, and distributed infra.
  • Meet every partner team the workstreams depend on; write down what each one is waiting for.
  • Find the decisions that are already slow, and ask the tech leads why. Change nothing yet.
Output: a one-page dependency map and a list of the three delays that cost the most compute.
60Install the rhythm
  • Stand up the experiment board (§3) against one workstream, not all of them.
  • Start the 30-minute weekly review with a fixed agenda: stalled runs, idle allocation, next gate.
  • Put allocation against realized utilization in front of the leads weekly — the conversation the numbers force is the point.
  • Kill anything I added that nobody reads by day 60. Process that needs defending is process that failed.
Output: one workstream running on the rhythm, with the leads asking for it rather than tolerating it.
90Carry a milestone
  • Publish launch criteria for a mid-training handoff into a frontier release — agreed before the run, not after.
  • Run the first milestone review and the first retro, then fix the format based on what failed.
  • Extend to the remaining workstreams only where the first one proved out.
  • Fold the allocation view into the portfolio picture I already own, so RL Scaling’s compute case is argued with evidence.
Output: one milestone delivered on the rhythm, and a compute ask backed by realized-utilization data.

The weekly, in 30 minutes

Fixed agenda · no status round-robin
  • Stalled runs first — anything past 7 days without a decision. Decide or kill it in the room.
  • Idle allocation — who is holding chips they aren’t realizing, and where it goes instead.
  • Next gate — one workstream, what it needs from whom.
  • Nothing else. Progress is on the board; reading it aloud is the tax I’m trying to remove.

Run retro

Four prompts · 15 minutes · every landed or killed run
What did we believe going in?
The hypothesis as written before results, not as remembered after.
Where did the signal actually change our mind?
Which checkpoint, which eval. Often much earlier than we acted on it.
What did the delay cost in chip-hours?
The number that makes the next decision faster.
What do we change in the next run’s plan?
One change. A retro producing six action items produces none.
